Frequently asked questions

Why isn't water content just 100 − Brix?

Because a refractometer's Brix scale is calibrated for sucrose, and honey is mostly fructose and glucose. The honey-specific correction subtracts an additional 1.6–1.8 % from (100 − Brix). TrueBrix applies that published offset and shows the formula on screen.

Which source is the table based on?

The Chataway (1932) refractive-index ↔ water-content table, as published in Hanna Instruments' Honiganalyse 2023 method guide (DIN 10752). The values were cross-checked against USDA Grades of Extracted Honey, 7 CFR Part 52 (Federal Register 1984), Table III.

What do the verdict colours mean?

At or below 18.6 % water is the USDA Grade A/B line (minimum 81.4 % soluble solids) — ready to cap and store. Above 18.6 % but at or below 20 % is still within the German honey-regulation limit but carries higher fermentation risk. Above 20 % is too wet.

Is my refractometer automatically temperature-compensated?

TrueBrix uses the reading you enter. If your refractometer has ATC it compensates internally; if not, measure near 20 °C or apply your instrument's correction. Temperature, calibration and sampling all affect the result.

How does the blend calculation work?

Moisture is a mass percentage, so mixing is a weighted average. For target T between batch A (a) and batch B (b): fraction of A = (T − b) / (a − b), fraction of B = 1 − fraction of A. The reverse mode computes T = r·a + (1 − r)·b. Mix by weight, not volume.
